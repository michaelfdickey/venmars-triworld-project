# Game Mechanics — VenMars: Tri-World Project

## Overview

The gameplay loop follows a Factorio-like progression: unlock technology → build production chains → scale throughput → unlock the next phase. The core difference is that instead of a factory floor, you manage industrial infrastructure across five celestial bodies and the transfer orbits between them.

---

## Time & Tick System

- **1 tick = 1 Earth day** (adjustable in settings: 1, 7, 30, 365 days/tick).
- Speed controls: Pause, 1×, 10×, 100×.
- Auto-pause triggers on milestone completion, critical failure, or funding crisis.
- Year counter starts at **2040**.

## Production Chain Design

Production follows directed acyclic graphs (DAGs). Each node is a **facility** that consumes inputs and produces outputs at a rate determined by its level and power supply.

### Example Chains

**Earth → Lunar Mass Driver**
```
Funds → Earth Launch → Payload in LEO
Payload in LEO → Lunar Transfer → Payload on Moon
Payload on Moon + Regolith → Assembly → Mass Driver Segment
8× Mass Driver Segment + Power Grid → Operational Mass Driver
```

**Lunar Surface Industry**
```
Regolith → Crusher → Crushed Ore
Crushed Ore → Smelter (⚡) → Steel + Aluminum + Silicon (byproducts)
Crushed Ore → Rare Earth Extractor (⚡) → Rare Earths + Waste Rock
Silicon → Solar Cell Fab (⚡) → Solar Panels
Steel + Rare Earths → Magnet Forge (⚡) → Superconducting Magnets
```

**Venus Atmospheric Harvesting**
```
Bulk Atmosphere → Ram Scoop Intake → Raw Gas
Raw Gas → Compressor (⚡) → Compressed Gas
Raw Gas → CO₂ Cracker (⚡) → Carbon + Oxygen
Carbon + Compressed Gas → Container Forge (⚡) → Sealed Container
Sealed Container → Mass Driver Launch → Container in Transit
```

**Mars Atmospheric Buildup**
```
Container in Transit → Atmospheric Entry → Gas Release + Surface Debris
Gas Release → Atmospheric Mixing (passive) → Pressure Increase
SO₂ Component → Acid Rain → Surface Contamination (negative)
Surface Debris → Salvage Drone → Recovered Materials (optional)
```

Each body has a set of buildable facilities. Facilities have:
- **Level** (1–10): determines throughput rate
- **Power requirement**: scales with level
- **Build cost**: resources + time
- **Staffing**: some facilities need population

## Victory Condition

Both planets must simultaneously maintain comfortable conditions for **10 consecutive Earth years**:

- **Venus**: Surface pressure 0.8–1.2 atm, surface temperature 0–40°C
- **Mars**: Surface pressure 0.8–1.2 atm, mean surface temperature 0–25°C

Score factors:
1. Calendar time from game start to victory
2. Total energy expended
3. Total mass launched from Earth (lower = better self-sufficiency)
4. Peak atmospheric escape rate on Mars (lower = better containment)
5. Sulfur contamination level on Mars (lower = cleaner)
6. Peak population across all off-Earth sites

## Failure Modes & Hazards

| Hazard | Trigger | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Funding Crisis | Earth spending exceeds GDP tolerance | Launches suspended, construction paused |
| Structural Collapse | Mass driver segment failure (random + age) | Facility offline, debris field |
| Platform Loss | Venus floating platform buoyancy failure | All facilities on that platform offline |
| Atmospheric Escape | Mars atmospheric stripping by solar wind | Net gas loss, pressure plateau |
| Sulfur Poisoning | Excess SO₂ delivered without scrubbing | Mars soil toxicity rises, habitability delayed |
| Container Stream Drift | Orbital perturbation by Jupiter/Venus | Containers miss Mars, mass wasted |
| Thermal Runaway | Greenhouse overshoot on Mars | Temperature exceeds target, must wait for equilibrium |
| Political Instability | Random events from Earth | Funding fluctuations, project delays |

## Economy & Funding

- Earth provides an annual **space budget** (starts at ~$50B/yr, grows with milestones and public interest).
- Each Earth launch costs funds based on payload mass and method.
- As off-world industry becomes self-sustaining, dependence on Earth funding decreases.
- **Public Interest meter**: major milestones boost funding; long stalls or disasters reduce it.
- Late-game: off-world colonies may generate their own economic output, reducing Earth dependency to zero.

## Research & Technology

Technology is not a traditional tech tree — instead, R&D improves existing systems:

| Research Area | Effect |
|---|---|
| Launch Vehicle Optimization | Reduces $/kg to orbit |
| Mass Driver Efficiency | Increases throughput, reduces power draw |
| Container Engineering | Increases gas capacity per container, reduces mass |
| Solar Energy | Improves panel efficiency, reduces area needed |
| Atmospheric Modeling | Better predictions, reduces risk of overshoot/undershoot |
| Materials Science | Stronger composites, longer facility lifespan |
| Orbital Mechanics | Reduces delta-v for transfers, widens launch windows |
| Life Support | Reduces resource cost per colonist |

R&D points are generated by Mission Control level + off-world research stations. Each research area has diminishing returns — early upgrades are cheap, late ones are expensive.

---

## Population & Workforce

- Early phases are fully robotic/automated.
- Habitats unlock human workers who **boost facility output by 50%** when staffed.
- Population grows via immigration from Earth (costs funds) and natural growth (very slow).
- Each person requires life support (power, water, food) — a resource drain.
- Population milestones unlock new capabilities and boost public interest.

---

## Orbital Mechanics Integration

- Venus–Mars transfer windows occur every ~334 days (synodic period).
- The player can launch containers outside optimal windows at a **delta-v penalty** (more energy, less efficient).
- Container flight time is ~6–12 months depending on trajectory.
- The player manages a **container pipeline**: containers launched in previous windows are still in transit while new ones launch.
- Late-game with enough mass drivers, thousands of containers are in flight simultaneously.

## UI Indicators Per Tab

### Atmosphere Sub-tab
- Pressure gauge (bar chart or dial)
- Temperature graph (current + projected)
- Gas composition breakdown (stacked bar)
- Habitability progress indicator

### Planetary Map Sub-tab
- 2D Mercator projection showing:
  - Facility locations
  - Impact zones (Mars)
  - Ice caps, significant terrain
  - Mass driver launch/landing corridors

### Production Sub-tab
- Factorio-style production chain viewer
- Per-facility: input/output rates, level, power, status
- Bottleneck highlighting (which facility is the slowest in a chain)

### Payload to Orbit Sub-tab
- Launch manifest (queued, in-flight, delivered)
- Mass throughput chart (tons/year)
- Launch window countdown (for Venus–Mars transfers)
- Container pipeline visualization

### Population Sub-tab
- Headcount and growth rate
- Life support resource consumption
- Worker assignment to facilities
- Immigration queue and cost

## Balance Philosophy

- **Early game is slow and expensive**: every launch from Earth costs real money, forcing careful prioritization.
- **Mid-game accelerates**: lunar mass driver enables cheap bulk transport, opening up Venus and asteroid operations.
- **Late game is a management challenge**: thousands of facilities across five bodies, massive container pipelines, and atmospheric models that take years to respond to changes.
- **No idle gameplay**: the simulation has real inertia. Atmospheric changes take decades. The player must plan ahead and commit to strategies, not react tick-by-tick.
